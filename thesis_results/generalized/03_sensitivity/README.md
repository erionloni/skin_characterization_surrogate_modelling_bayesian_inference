# Generalized route: sensitivity

Sobol sensitivity of the six pressures to the four material and three protocol inputs, material/protocol group effects, interactions and support audit; the Sobol indices of 26 derived features of the pressures, ranked per input.

The GP Sobol indices are Monte Carlo estimates (40,000 samples per matrix, recorded seed) with an error visible in the values: five slightly negative first-order indices (hold and pause at D6, down to -0.006), seven first-order indices above their totals (by up to 0.005), and material x protocol second-order indices with an offset per output (mean about -0.004 at D2 and +0.008 at D6, every D6 pair at least +0.006), which is estimator error, not interaction. Indices of that size are zero within their error. The tables keep the estimates as computed, negative values included; only the interaction gap, max(S_T - S_1, 0), sets a negative difference to 0. Second-order indices are read per output and never averaged over outputs. The group sums of gp_material_protocol_group_summary.csv add the offsets of their pairs (material_protocol_second_order_sum: 12 pairs). The PCE indices are analytic.

The PCE ratio-feature indices (`data/supporting/ratio_sobol_pce_total.csv`) are kept as computed but not drawn. The ratios are formed point by point from one prediction of the six pressures, as for the GP, but the PCE extrapolates `pcl1_D6_mbar` to zero and below in a few corners of the seven-input box (on 20,000 uniform points its minimum is -0.85 mbar, the GP's 38 mbar). The D6 ratios are then heavy-tailed (-19 to 8; GP 0.45 to 1.11) and their Monte Carlo indices depend on a few extreme points (total indices summing to 1.68 and 2.08 per ratio; GP 1.12 and 1.07). The GP ratio-feature indices (`ratio_sobol_gp_total`) are the ones to read.

A rerun with the fixed-surrogate bootstrap (settings B.4) also writes `fixed_surrogate_bootstrap_*.csv`: a bootstrap of its own Monte Carlo sample of 5,000 points with Jansen's total-order estimator. Its intervals describe the Monte Carlo error of that sample's estimates, not of the point estimates in `data/primary/` (UQLab: another sample, 40,000 points, Janon's estimator). In the thesis run the bootstrap's sample lies 2.7 to 3.7 standard deviations (of independent 5,000-point samples) high for m3_e,RD in all six outputs and 2.7 for m3_f,RD in pcl1_D2_mbar: these are the 7 of the 42 GP total-order point estimates outside its 95 % intervals. The two estimators differ by at most 0.008 on one sample. The thesis results do not ship these intervals.

The derived features (the files starting with `features_`, step `feature_sensitivity`): the first- and total-order Sobol indices of the derived features of the six pressures in the feature catalog of the settings (0.7), from the GP mean, with the estimator, sample and seed of the derived-feature indices. Families: F1 ratios (within a probe, between the probes q, double ratios R), F2 differences (mbar), F3 levels (geometric means), F4 decline shape (curvature s and drop ratios; exploratory). Log-linear features are used as log f, differences and drop ratios raw; a feature that the step sensitivity already computes keeps its raw definition and its indices there (`tables/features_registry.csv`: the definition, convention, status and physical hypothesis of each feature). F0 shows the primary indices of the six pressures. Every feature is a function of the same six pressures.

A total-order index is the share of a feature's variation that a parameter explains, interactions included, not the size of that variation: the effect size sqrt(ST x variance) gives the size in the feature's own units (log units read as relative change; mbar for differences). Neither says whether the parameter can be recovered from a measurement. The existing Sobol path gives no confidence intervals. A drop ratio whose denominator changes sign over the sample, or whose variance a few samples carry (settings 0.7), is UNSTABLE: reported, not ranked (in the thesis results: dropratio_D2 and dropratio_D6). The rankings mark the features of the observation design physics_guided_full_rank_six (pcl3_D2_mbar, r31_D2, d21_D2, r31_D6, r21_D6; its fourth feature, pcl1_D2_mbar / pcl3_D6_mbar, has no catalog entry).

The double ratio R = R21 = (pcl2_D2_mbar / pcl1_D2_mbar) / (pcl2_D6_mbar / pcl1_D6_mbar). Under independent 8 % relative errors of the six pressures, SD(log R) = 2c = 16 %. Decision record: "WP5, 26 Sep 2026. No inverse test with the double ratio R. log R is a linear combination of the within-probe log-ratios (row 1 minus row 3 of the contrast matrix A). Any valid observation design containing R is informationally identical to ratios_only or all_six, or discards information. Appending R to an existing design double counts and has a singular covariance. R could only change inference under a noise model with cross-probe, cycle-dependent correlation. Such a model is outside the scope of this thesis; measuring the device's noise correlations is an outlook item. R is reported as a sensitivity result and as a reconstruction diagnostic in WP3." (WP3: the ratio-error curves, `fixed/08_ratio_error_curves`.)

On this route the gap ST - S1 of a feature also contains the interactions between the material and the protocol inputs.
